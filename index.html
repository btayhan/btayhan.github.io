      What I've Done So Far

      I started in Molecular Biology and Genetics at Yıldız Technical University, where I completed my B.Sc. in 2021. During my M.Sc. in Molecular Biology, Genetics, and Bioengineering at Sabancı University, I moved toward computational biophysics and joined MIDSTLab. I completed my M.Sc. in 2025 and I am now continuing in the same program as a doctoral student.

      Since joining MIDSTLab, I have mainly worked on two problems. The first is the conformational dynamics of calmodulin (CaM), where I used classical molecular dynamics and well-tempered metadynamics to study how calcium binding and ionic strength reshape its free-energy landscape. This work developed into our 2026 paper in the Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling. The second is DHFR mutational scanning, where I have worked with large-scale alchemical free energy perturbation (FEP) simulations on HPC resources to investigate how mutations change inhibitor preference. Our first FEP pipeline study was published in 2025, and I am continuing this project by comparing FEP-derived relative binding with experimental fitness data.

      I have also presented different stages of these projects at scientific meetings through talks and posters. In 2025, I presented our CaM work as a selected talk at HIBIT 2025, and in 2026 this work was published in the Journal of Chemical Information and Modeling. My next presentation will be at ECCB 2026 in Geneva, where I will present our latest DHFR mutational-scanning results as a poster.

      What I'm Working On

      My research sits at the intersection of molecular dynamics, enhanced sampling, free-energy calculations, protein dynamics, and high-performance computing. I am interested in how environmental changes and mutations reshape protein conformational ensembles, and how those changes are reflected in molecular recognition, inhibitor binding, and function.

      For CaM, I used physically interpretable collective variables with well-tempered metadynamics to map calcium- and salt-dependent conformational landscapes and connected the sampled states with experimentally observed CaM–target complexes. For DHFR, I use GPU-accelerated FEP simulations and automated analysis workflows to build mutational landscapes of relative inhibitor binding and compare them with experimental fitness measurements.
